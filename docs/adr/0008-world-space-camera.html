ADR-0008: World Space + Deadzone Camera + Viewport Culling

Accepted · 2026-08-02 · Author: ticket 013 session

Context — why this decision is needed

Session slots stack from the top in the right-hand column at 288px intervals (sprite 240 + label band 48). If the viewport height is 800px, 2.8 is the limit — and yet this game's premise is "every session alive right now on this machine," and in practice it is not rare for one person to have 10 or more running.

In the previous structure the coordinate system was stage-local = canvas pixels = viewport, all three being one and the same, and StageGeometry.stageHeight was simply the viewport height. Overflowing slots went outside the canvas and just vanished. The player's vertical movement was also clamped inside the viewport, so from the 4th session onward they were neither on screen nor hittable. The requirement that "characters line up in proportion to the number of sessions" had quietly been broken.

At the same time there is a constraint in the opposite direction. One character is one Lottie animation, and every frame costs a frame() seek plus rasterization. If there are 30 sessions and only 3 of them are on screen, running all 30 is pure waste.

Decision — what was decided

  1. Promote the coordinate system to "world." World height = max(viewport height, session count × slot pitch + margin). Slots keep stacking downward beyond the viewport, and the player's vertical clamp is also world-based. The concept of a floor (a fixed ground) has been removed — as long as the world has a variable height, a "ground" does not hold (the decorative floor line was removed too). The worker contract's stageHeight has changed in both name and meaning to worldHeight.
  2. The camera is a single render-only offset (render/camera.ts). screen.y = world.y - camera.y. The simulation (game/) does not know the camera exists — hit detection happens 100% in world coordinates, so the result is the same no matter where the screen is looking.
  3. Following uses a deadzone approach. While the player's screen y is inside the 25%~75% band of the viewport vertically, the camera is completely still. Once it leaves the band, the camera follows with exponential decay (1 - exp(-dt/τ), τ=90ms) so as to stick back to that boundary, and it is clamped to [0, worldHeight - viewportHeight].
  4. Targets outside the viewport are detached from the scene (culling). Rather than just skipping drawing, targetLayer.remove(scene) makes thorvg not even traverse them, and the animation clock (tick) is stopped as well. When they come back in, they are restored with add. The margin is one slot (288px), so they are already attached before appearing on screen.
  5. DOM labels are translated as one whole dedicated layer. Instead of writing coordinates into each of the N labels, a single translate3d(0, -cameraY, 0) is applied to the .session-labels container.

Rationale — why it was done that way

Why the camera was not put into the simulation: the worker cannot see the DOM (ADR-0003), and the viewport size is a render-side fact. If the camera went into the simulation, hit detection would come to depend on "where the screen is looking right now," and the precise hitboxes painstakingly tuned in ticket 012 would wobble depending on scroll state. Keeping it render-only makes the camera a purely visual effect, and in fact, thanks to this decision, hitting the bottom-most character of 12 sessions (world y=2760, a position that only becomes visible once the camera has gone down to 2160) worked as-is with no special handling.

Why a deadzone (user decision): if the camera always pins the player to the center of the screen, the whole background shakes along with even left/right movement or tiny up/down movement, which makes you motion-sick. The 25% band matches the intuition that "the screen stays still until you touch the edge."

Why exponential decay was chosen: fixed-speed tracking stops abruptly at the moment of arrival, and a fixed per-frame ratio (lerp(0.15)) approach changes speed with the frame rate. 1 - exp(-dt/τ) converges by the same amount in the same time whether at 60Hz or 144Hz. Measured: at a movement speed of 300px/s, the player pushes ~20px past the band before the camera catches up.

Why culling by detaching from the scene was chosen: the alternative is leaving them at alpha 0, but then thorvg still traverses the nodes and prepares transform/compositing. Whether remove/add is possible was decided by checking the bindings — Scene.add explicitly calls _tvg_paint_ref() internally and remove undoes that reference, so the refcount stays balanced (the JS wrapper holds one more reference of its own, so remove does not destroy the native object). Measured with 12 sessions · an 800px viewport: 4~7 targets attached to the scene depending on camera position, with the remaining 5~8 detached — that is, even as sessions grow, the frame cost is proportional only to the number visible on screen.

The z-order among targets does get shuffled by remove/add, but since slots do not overlap vertically it is not visible. The guarantee that the player is always on top is not broken either — the player is attached to the canvas after the target layer, and what culling touches is inside the layer (the ADR-0006 rule "do not detach and reattach paints for the sake of draw order" pays off here).

Alternatives — considered but rejected

  • Grow the canvas to the world height and scroll the page — canvas pixels explode in proportion to the session count (30 sessions = 7200px tall), it hits the browser's maximum canvas size, and rasterization cost is incurred in full even for invisible regions.
  • Shrink the slot spacing when there are many sessions to cram them all in — characters overlap, and as the slot pitch diverges from the sprite size the hitbox rules (ticket 012) would have to be reworked.
  • Have the simulation own the camera — detection would come to depend on the viewport (see above).
  • Center-pinned follow (no deadzone) — the user explicitly chose the deadzone.
  • alpha 0 without culling — the traversal cost remains.

Consequences — the results of this decision

  • However many sessions there are, they all exist and can all be hit. The screen shows only the range the camera picked.
  • Frame cost is proportional to the number of sessions visible on screen, not the number of sessions.
  • Because a culled node's clock stops, when it becomes visible again the idle loop phase continues (which can look like a reset). It is a trade-off accepted in ticket 013.
  • ⚠️ Exit interacts with culling. A culled node has its tick stopped, so expired never arrives, which means if a session ends while culled the node remains forever. This was prevented by putting it back into the scene in #beginExit so it takes the normal fade path (a bug we actually hit).
  • Since the floor line is gone, the spawn position became "the vertical center of the viewport" — being right in the middle of the deadzone, the camera never moves right after boot.
  • Revisit condition: if the world also grows horizontally (targets appearing on both sides, etc.), the camera must be extended to two dimensions. Right now camera.ts deliberately has only a single vertical offset.
